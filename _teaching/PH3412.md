### Overview

Most of the visible matter in the universe is in the plasma state: the solar corona and solar wind, planetary magnetospheres and ionospheres, the interstellar medium, pulsars, and astrophysical jets. Understanding these systems requires the physics of charged particles and magnetised fluids. This course introduces the physics of plasmas in astrophysical and space environments, from plasma fundamentals and charged-particle motion to magnetohydrodynamics, plasma waves, magnetic reconnection, instabilities, shocks, particle acceleration, dynamo action, and turbulence. Intended for Year 3 and Year 4 physics students, the course would equip the students with a conceptual and problem-solving foundation for further study in astrophysics, plasma physics, and space science.

### Course Contents
1. Plasma fundamentals
   - Quasineutrality, Debye shielding, and plasma frequency
   - Key plasma parameters in astrophysical and space environments
   - Fluid and kinetic viewpoints of plasmas
2. Charged-particle motion
   - Cyclotron motion, gyrofrequency, and Larmor radius
   - Guiding-centre approximation, E×B, grad-B, and curvature drifts
   - Magnetic moment, adiabatic invariants, and magnetic mirroring
3. Magnetohydrodynamics (MHD)
   - Fluid description of plasmas and the MHD approximation
   - Ideal MHD and the frozen-in condition
   - Magnetic pressure, magnetic tension, and magnetic Reynolds number
4. Plasma waves
   - Alfvén and magnetosonic waves
   - Langmuir and ion-acoustic waves
   - Waves in the solar wind, corona, and magnetosphere
5. Magnetic reconnection and instabilities
   - Magnetic reconnection and plasma energy conversion
   - Solar flares and magnetotail reconnection
   - Selected instabilities in astrophysical systems
6. Shocks and particle acceleration
   - Collisionless shocks in heliospheric and astrophysical environments
   - Particle acceleration and non-thermal radiation
   - Supernova remnants, pulsars, and astrophysical jets
7. Dynamo and turbulence
   - Dynamo action and magnetic-field generation in planets and stars
   - Plasma turbulence and energy cascades
   - The solar wind as a plasma laboratory
8. Applications
   - Solar, magnetospheric, and interstellar plasmas, ionosphere, space weather, observational missions, etc
