Plasma core

Energetic particles in fusion plasmas

Energetic particles heat the plasma and drive current, but they also interact with turbulence and can help suppress it. Using first-principles gyrokinetic simulations with GYSELA, this project studies how energetic particles, turbulence and modes such as EGAMs and Alfvén eigenmodes interact, and what that means for confinement.

  • Turbulence
  • Gyrokinetics
  • GYSELA
  • Alfvén eigenmodes
  • EGAMs

Core → edge

Impurity transport

Impurities that build up in the core dilute the fuel and radiate energy away. This project uses gyrokinetic simulations with GYSELA to find how turbulence, collisions and magnetic geometry drive impurity fluxes, peaking and redistribution for different species and operating regimes.

  • Gyrokinetics
  • GYSELA
  • Neoclassical transport

Edge, SOL & divertor

Edge plasma turbulence

Turbulence at the plasma edge sets how heat and particles reach the wall. With three-dimensional fluid modelling, this project studies edge and scrape-off-layer turbulence, detachment and exhaust, blobs, plasma–neutral interactions and alternative divertor configurations.

  • Turbulence
  • 3D fluid modelling
  • Scrape-off layer
  • Divertor & exhaust
  • Blobs

Whole plasma

Reduced transport model

Full turbulence simulations are too expensive for wide design scans. This project builds machine-learning surrogate models, including deep and physics-informed neural networks, trained on large databases of gyrokinetic simulations and discharges, to predict plasma performance in seconds.
